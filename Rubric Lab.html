Practice scenario: Lake Arlen offsite
“Plan a three-day offsite, Thursday Mar 12 to Saturday Mar 14, 2026, for a team of 8 at Lake Arlen. Use the attached venue list, team profile, policy manual and events calendar. Deliver a PDF named Offsite Agenda 2026 with each day on its own page. Every session, meal and transfer is its own row with time, venue, vendor ID, cost per person, party total, transport and notes. End with a budget summary for meals, activities, transport and lodging. Costs use the per-person rates multiplied by 8. Follow the policy manual on allergies, closures and the budget.”

    The checker spots wording patterns; it can’t judge meaning. A clean result means “re-read it once more,” and a flagged “and” can be fine when both parts form one required item.

    Final checklist

    Run these seven checks on the whole rubric before you submit it.

      Good criteria can still add up to the wrong rubric.

      Line-by-line review catches wording problems. This list catches what only shows when you read the rubric as a whole: duplicates, gaps, missing penalties and items a strong response could fail.

      Two stress tests

      • Would a flawed response pass this criterion? Then it is too weak.
      • Would a good response that takes another valid route fail it? Then it is too narrow.
